\section{Nanopb Protocol over UART and SPI}

\subsection{Executive Summary}

\textbf{Overview:} Protocol Buffers (nanopb) encapsulated in length-prefixed,
CRC-32-protected frames. A single wire format is shared across two transports:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{UART} (primary, gateway link): RX72N SCI9 $\rightarrow$
        CY7C65213 USB-UART bridge $\rightarrow$ Raspberry Pi 5
        \texttt{/dev/ttyUSB0} at 921\,600 baud. Carries every frame type
        including protobuf commands, telemetry, heartbeats, and
        \texttt{LOG\_MESSAGE} payloads that multiplex the RX72N's runtime log
        stream onto the same byte stream as protocol traffic.
  \item \textbf{SPI} (dedicated ROS2 link): RSPI2 $\rightarrow$
        \texttt{/dev/spidev0.0} at 10\,MHz, full-duplex. Terminated on the Pi5
        by the \texttt{star\_spi\_bridge} ROS2 node for motor-control and
        telemetry paths that need sub-millisecond latency. HARQ (Chase
        Combining over a rate-$\frac{1}{2}$ K=7 convolutional code) protects
        against motor-driver EMI.
\end{itemize}

Native USB (USB0) on the RX72N has been removed from the system: its driver
stack was not reliable. The UART-to-USB bridge is the sole USB-class
connection between the board and the Pi5.

\subsection{Frame Format}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{SYNC} & \textbf{SEQ} & \textbf{LEN} & \textbf{TYPE} & \textbf{FLAGS} & \textbf{PAYLOAD} & \textbf{CRC-32} \\
2\,B (LE)     & 2\,B (LE)    & 2\,B (LE)    & 1\,B          & 1\,B           & 0--1024\,B       & 4\,B (LE) \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SYNC}: fixed \texttt{0x55AA}, wire bytes \texttt{0xAA 0x55}.
  \item \textbf{SEQ}: 16-bit monotonic sequence number (wraps at 65535).
  \item \textbf{LEN}: payload length in bytes, $0 \le \mathtt{LEN} \le 1024$.
  \item \textbf{TYPE}: frame-type discriminator (see below).
  \item \textbf{FLAGS}: bitmask (\texttt{REQUIRES\_ACK}, \texttt{RETRANSMIT},
        \texttt{PRIORITY}, \texttt{FEC\_ENABLED}, \texttt{SOFT\_NACK}).
  \item \textbf{PAYLOAD}: nanopb-encoded protobuf message (for data frames) or
        raw ASCII (for \texttt{LOG\_MESSAGE}).
  \item \textbf{CRC-32}: IEEE~802.3 polynomial \texttt{0x04C11DB7} computed
        over SYNC + header + payload.
\end{itemize}

Minimum frame: 12 bytes (header + CRC, empty payload). Maximum: 1036 bytes.

\subsection{Frame Types}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|l|l|}
\hline
\textbf{TYPE} & \textbf{Name}        & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\hline
\texttt{0x00} & \texttt{PING}        & Liveness probe (either direction) \\
\texttt{0x01} & \texttt{PONG}        & Liveness reply echoing payload \\
\texttt{0x10} & \texttt{COMMAND}     & Pi5 $\rightarrow$ RX72N, protobuf command \\
\texttt{0x11} & \texttt{RESPONSE}    & RX72N $\rightarrow$ Pi5, protobuf response / telemetry \\
\texttt{0x12} & \texttt{ACK}         & SPI-only positive acknowledgment \\
\texttt{0x13} & \texttt{NACK}        & SPI-only negative acknowledgment \\
\texttt{0x20} & \texttt{LOG\_MESSAGE} & RX72N $\rightarrow$ Pi5, ASCII log chunk \\
\texttt{0xFE} & \texttt{RESET\_ACK}  & Session reset acknowledgment \\
\texttt{0xFF} & \texttt{RESET}       & Session reset request \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection{LOG\_MESSAGE (\texttt{0x20})}

The RX72N's \texttt{rx\_log\_*} macros enqueue ASCII bytes into an in-RAM ring
buffer (\texttt{rx\_log\_uart.c}). The communication task drains the ring on
every 10\,ms poll tick, wraps the drained bytes in a \texttt{LOG\_MESSAGE}
frame, and transmits over UART. The gateway's frame dispatcher
(\texttt{manager.go}) extracts the payload and prints it to
\texttt{stderr}/log file with an \texttt{[RX72N]} prefix.

Framing the log bytes (rather than writing raw ASCII) prevents the log text
from containing the \texttt{0x55AA} sync word and fooling the frame decoder.

\subsection{CRC and Error Handling}

CRC-32 is computed once per frame and mismatched frames are discarded. On SPI,
the link layer (\texttt{rx\_spi\_link}) additionally runs a Chase-Combining
HARQ loop with rate-$\frac{1}{2}$ convolutional FEC and Viterbi decoding to
recover from EMI bursts. UART relies on the CRC alone plus bounded resync
(\texttt{rx\_frame\_decode\_with\_resync}); the bridge chip and USB CDC
transport on the host provide sufficient lossless transport for the gateway
path.
